## Purpose
This sheet provides the minimum training staff need to use AI safely and responsibly in the workplace. It supports the organisation’s AI policy and reduces the risk of data leakage, poor output quality, unsafe automation and unmanaged AI use.

AI is a productivity tool, not an authority. Staff are accountable for the prompts they use, the outputs they rely on and the decisions they act upon.

## Training requirement
All staff who use AI must complete this training before using approved AI tools in business processes. Managers must ensure users understand the approved boundaries, escalation steps and responsibilities for safe AI use.

## Approved use
Use only AI tools approved by the business.

Examples may include:

- Microsoft 365 Copilot for approved business scenarios
- GitHub Copilot for approved software development tasks
- approved enterprise or business-tier model services for defined use cases
- approved AI agents and workflows with named owners

Do not use personal or free-trial AI accounts for company work without written approval.

## What not to paste into AI
Never paste any of the following into an AI tool unless the tool, context and use case are explicitly approved:

- passwords, secrets, API keys, tokens or certificates
- payroll, banking or HR information
- customer or client personal data
- contracts, legal records or privileged information
- unreleased IP, source code or product plans containing sensitive details
- regulated health, financial or compliance data
- any content you are not permitted to share outside the business

If you are unsure, do not paste it.

## Safe use rules
- Use AI only for approved business tasks.
- Keep prompts limited to the business purpose and do not include unrelated data.
- Review every output before sharing, publishing, sending or acting on it.
- Verify facts, figures, references, legal statements and code before relying on them.
- Keep a human accountable for any decision that affects staff, customers, legal obligations or external commitments.
- Do not ask AI to bypass controls, hide data or avoid review.

## Common risks

### 1. Hallucinations
AI may produce confident but incorrect output. Always validate important information before relying on it.

### 2. Prompt injection
External content may be designed to influence an AI system. Treat email, websites, uploaded files and external data as untrusted.

### 3. Oversharing
Do not provide confidential or sensitive information to unapproved AI tools.

### 4. Unsafe automation
AI agents and workflows must never take irreversible actions without human approval.

### 5. Source code risk
AI-generated code can create security flaws, licensing issues or poor quality. Always review and test before use.

## Microsoft 365 Copilot rules
If you use Microsoft 365 Copilot:

- use it only for approved work scenarios
- do not enter Restricted or Never data unless the specific use has been approved
- review any generated draft or summary before sending externally
- follow the organisation’s data classification and sharing rules
- do not use Copilot in a way that increases exposure of internal or sensitive content

## GitHub Copilot rules
If you use GitHub Copilot:

- do not paste secrets, credentials or sensitive code into prompts
- review generated code before merging, testing or deploying it
- validate security, licensing and quality issues before use
- never rely on AI output without review and testing

## AI agent and workflow rules
If you build or use an AI agent:

- use only approved tools and connectors
- apply least-privilege access only
- ensure irreversible actions require an approval step
- report unexpected behaviour immediately
- confirm that the agent has a named owner and a clear purpose

## Incident reporting
Report immediately if:

- you entered restricted or sensitive data into an unapproved AI tool
- an AI output appears incorrect, unsafe or misleading
- an AI agent acted unexpectedly, misrouted data or sent content incorrectly
- a tool appears to be collecting or exposing company data without approval
- a secret, token or credential was exposed through AI use

Report to: [contact]

## Quick self-check before using AI
Ask yourself:

- Is the tool approved for this task?
- Is the data class allowed for this tool?
- Am I using a business account and an approved environment?
- Am I allowed to share this data with the tool?
- Have I validated the output before relying on it?
- Would I be comfortable explaining this action to the business owner or security lead?
- If this output were shared externally, would it still be acceptable?
